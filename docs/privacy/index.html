Privacy Policy

Thymer Google Calendar ("the plugin") is a plugin for the Thymer note-taking app that keeps your Google Calendar and your Thymer workspace in two-way sync. It runs entirely inside your own Thymer application. It is developed by Parham Shafti. Last updated: 3 October 2026.

1. What Google user data the plugin accesses

Only after you sign in with Google and grant consent, the plugin accesses the following data through the Google Calendar API, using two scopes:

  • Your calendar list (scope calendar.calendarlist.readonly): the names, colours, IDs and access levels of the calendars you are subscribed to, and the e-mail address of the Google account you connected.
  • Events in the calendars you choose to sync (scope calendar.events), within a time window you set: title, description, start and end time, location, status, attendees and organizer (names, e-mail addresses and response status), video meeting links, the event's link and ID, and its last-updated time.

The plugin does not access any other Google data, such as Gmail, Contacts or Drive, and it does not access calendars you have not selected.

2. How the plugin uses Google user data

  • To show your events as pages in your Thymer workspace and keep them up to date.
  • To send the changes you make in Thymer back to Google Calendar: creating an event when you create an event page with a time and a calendar, and updating its time, location, your response, attendees or video meeting link when you edit that page. The plugin never deletes events and never changes calendar settings or sharing.
  • To link attendees and organizers to pages in a people collection of your choice, matched by e-mail address and name, and, if you enable it, to create a page with the person's name and e-mail address.
  • To show the list of your calendars so you can choose which ones to sync.

Google user data is used only to provide these features to you. It is not used for advertising, profiling, analytics or market research, it is not sold, and it is not used to develop, improve or train any artificial intelligence or machine learning models.

3. With whom Google user data is shared, transferred or disclosed

  • Google user data is stored only in your own Thymer workspace, through Thymer's own storage and sync, so it is visible only to you and to people you have chosen to share that workspace with in Thymer.
  • The developer of the plugin does not receive, store or have access to any of your Google user data. The plugin has no servers of its own.
  • Google user data is not shared, transferred or disclosed to any third party, except to Google itself when the plugin writes your changes back to Google Calendar.
  • Optional place suggestions: when you type in an event's Location field, the text you type is sent to Geoapify, or to OpenStreetMap's Photon search (photon.komoot.io) when Geoapify is unavailable, to look up matching places. Only the text you type is sent; no data received from Google is sent with it. You can turn place suggestions off in the plugin's settings.

The plugin's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

4. How Google user data is protected

  • All communication with Google uses encrypted HTTPS (TLS) connections directly between your device and Google.
  • Sign-in uses Google's OAuth 2.0 with PKCE. Your Google password is never seen by the plugin.
  • The access and refresh tokens are stored only on your device, in the Thymer application's local storage, separately for each Thymer workspace. They are never sent anywhere except to Google.
  • The sign-in return pages on this website (/oauth/) only pass Google's one-time sign-in code back into your Thymer application. They do not store, log or forward any data.
  • The plugin requests only the two narrowest scopes it needs and cannot delete events or change calendar settings.

5. How long Google user data is kept, and how to delete it

  • Tokens are kept on your device until you disconnect the account. Disconnecting an account in the plugin's settings revokes its tokens with Google and deletes them from the device. You can also remove the plugin's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Synced events stay as pages in your Thymer workspace for as long as you keep them, like any other page you own. You can delete them at any time by deleting the pages or the Calendar collection in Thymer. Deleting a page in Thymer does not delete the event in Google.
  • A small local sync index (event IDs matched to page IDs, without event content) is kept in the Thymer application's local storage on each device so the same event is not imported twice. It stays until you clear Thymer's app data on that device.
  • The developer keeps no copy of any Google user data, so there is nothing to delete on the developer's side. If you have questions about your data, contact the address below.
